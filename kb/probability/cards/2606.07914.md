problem: >
  When can the latent component distributions, and the mixing matrix, of several
  unlabeled finite mixtures that share components but have unknown mixing weights
  be recovered, using only the assumption that each component is independent on at
  least one coordinate pair?
setting: >
  Several observable mixture distributions over the same latent components with
  unknown weights. No labels, no clean component samples, no known proportions, no
  parametric component families. Each component assumed marginally independent for
  at least one pair of coordinates. Both a population identifiability question and
  a finite-sample estimation question are treated; experiments on synthetic data
  and flow cytometry.
method: >
  Structural identifiability via a subset-rank condition on the spans of the
  univariate marginals: any fully independent distribution in the affine span of
  product-form components must coincide with a single component, so the
  intersection of the affine mixture space with the independence model is
  discrete. This is extended to coordinate-pair (marginal) independence under
  no-cancellation and full-rank conditions. Estimation uses a Product-Marginal
  Maximum Mean Discrepancy criterion (comparing a candidate's bivariate marginal
  with the product of its one-dimensional marginals via a characteristic kernel),
  minimized over signed affine combinations of the observable mixtures. Kernel
  mean embedding deviation bounds give uniform convergence and stability under
  approximate marginal independence.
contribution: >
  Identifiability theorem for mixtures of marginally independent components from
  unlabeled multi-mixtures, recovering components and (under completion
  conditions) the mixing matrix; the PM-MMD estimator with proven uniform
  convergence and stability; a clean separation of the empirical status of the
  assumptions (marginal independence yields a held-out candidate-level diagnostic,
  whereas irreducibility is not testable from unlabeled mixtures alone).

# Identifiability and Estimation for Unlabeled Finite Mixtures under Marginal Independence

Recovers latent components and the mixing matrix from several unlabeled mixtures
with unknown weights, using only marginal (pairwise) independence of each
component as the identifying signal. Population identifiability rests on
affine-span subset-rank and no-cancellation conditions; estimation uses a
Product-Marginal MMD criterion with uniform convergence via kernel mean embedding
concentration bounds.
