problem: >
  Estimating high-dimensional generalized structural equation models (equivalently
  generalized linear latent variable models) is computationally hard: the observed-data
  likelihood is a p-dimensional integral over the latent space, full-information
  maximum likelihood is intractable for discrete indicators, and there is no practical
  algorithm for outcome families such as Poisson, negative binomial, or gamma. How can
  the structural regression coefficients be estimated at scale, stably, for arbitrary
  GLM outcome families?
setting: >
  n independent subjects; a p-vector of latent predictors eta ~ N(0, Phi) measured by a
  J-vector of mixed binary/ordinal/continuous indicators through a linear factor
  measurement model Z = nu + Lambda eta + epsilon; an outcome y whose conditional mean
  given eta follows a GLM (or quasi-likelihood family) with link g and structural
  coefficient vector beta. High-dimensional latent space is the regime of interest.
  Frequentist estimation; evaluation by Monte Carlo simulation plus a Big Five Inventory
  application with ordinal indicators. No asymptotic theory presented in the available
  sections.
method: >
  Two-Stage Quasi-Likelihood EM (TSQLEM), a limited-information estimator that separates
  the measurement model from the structural model. Stage 1 approximates the conditional
  distribution of the latent variables given the observed indicators, eta | Z. Stage 2
  derives the conditional first and second moments of the outcome given the indicators,
  y | Z, either in closed form or by Monte Carlo integration, and plugs them into
  marginal quasi-score estimating equations for the structural parameters, solved by
  iteratively reweighted least squares. The full joint likelihood is never evaluated.
contribution: >
  Introduces the TSQLEM framework: by decoupling the measurement model from the
  structural model and estimating the structural coefficients from marginal quasi-score
  equations built on conditional moments of y | Z, it makes high-dimensional GSEM
  estimation numerically stable and fast, and extends tractable estimation to
  quasi-likelihood outcome families (Poisson, negative binomial, gamma) that lack
  existing algorithms. Simulations report large runtime reductions and lower MSE /
  structural bias relative to full- and limited-information competitors.

# Two-Stage Quasi-Likelihood EM for High-Dimensional Generalized SEM

Estimator paper. TSQLEM splits a generalized structural equation model into a
measurement stage (conditional latent distribution eta | Z) and a structural stage that
solves marginal quasi-score equations built from conditional moments of y | Z, evaluated
exactly or by Monte Carlo and iterated with IRLS. Avoids the full joint likelihood
integral, scales to high latent dimension, and handles arbitrary GLM/quasi-likelihood
outcome families. Simulations plus a Big Five application. Methods and conclusion
sections were not available and no theory is stated in the extract; card at low
confidence.
