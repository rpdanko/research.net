problem: >
  How can one estimate the parameters of a time-scaled inhomogeneous phase-type (IPH)
  distribution when the underlying multi-state process is observed only at discrete,
  irregularly spaced time points, so that transition times and the absorption time are
  latent and estimation is a missing-data problem?
setting: >
  Continuous-time finite-state inhomogeneous Markov jump process whose sub-intensity
  matrix has the time-scaled form Lambda(t) = h_beta(t) Lambda, so the process maps to a
  homogeneous Markov jump process under a deterministic time change. Panel / multi-state
  data: only the state at discrete irregular observation times is recorded; exact jump
  and absorption times are unobserved. Parametric families studied are matrix-Gompertz
  and matrix-Weibull. Frequentist likelihood-based inference; simulation plus a coronary
  allograft vasculopathy (CAV) progression application.
method: >
  A Stochastic EM algorithm with Markov-bridge data augmentation. Each iteration:
  (simulation) simulate the latent continuous-time trajectory conditional on the
  observed discrete states using Markov-bridge sampling on the time-transformed
  homogeneous process; (maximization) update the baseline sub-intensity matrix Lambda by
  its closed-form complete-data maximum-likelihood estimator, and refine the
  time-scaling parameter beta by gradient ascent on the same complete-data
  log-likelihood. Reported estimates come from complete-data MLE updates, avoiding
  constrained nonlinear optimization of the observed-data likelihood.
contribution: >
  Gives the first inference procedure for time-scaled IPH distributions under discrete,
  irregular observation of the latent jump process, combining Markov-bridge data
  augmentation with Stochastic EM so that every parameter update is a closed-form or
  gradient complete-data MLE step. Establishes identifiability conditions separating the
  baseline sub-intensity matrix from the time-scaling function, and validates on
  matrix-Gompertz / matrix-Weibull simulations and CAV progression data with a
  homogeneous-model comparison.

# Time-Scaled Inhomogeneous Phase-Type Estimation from Panel Data

Estimator paper. Fits time-scaled IPH distributions to interval-censored multi-state
data via Stochastic EM with Markov-bridge data augmentation on the time-transformed
homogeneous jump process. Closed-form complete-data MLE for the baseline sub-intensity
matrix, gradient ascent for the time-scaling parameter, plus identifiability conditions.
Simulations (matrix-Gompertz, matrix-Weibull) and a CAV application. Conclusion and
methods sections were not in the extract; card at medium confidence.
