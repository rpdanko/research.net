  of microscopic configurations; a single scalar control parameter (cellular
  tension, taken to increase with substrate stiffness) is varied. Model
  predictions are compared against measured correlation length and anisotropy
  in senescent human fibroblasts across a stiffness range. Only the abstract

method: >
  Build a statistical-mechanical model in which the free energy balances
  configurational entropy of the filament/bundle arrangement against
  mechanically dependent interaction energies between cytoskeletal elements.
  Sweep the tension control parameter and locate the parameter values at which
  the favoured ensemble changes character. The model yields three successive
  structural transitions identified with stress-fibre formation, stress-fibre
  alignment, and lateral aggregation of fibres, and predicts how correlation
  length and orientational anisotropy evolve along the tension axis.

  fibroblasts, and its preservation despite altered cell physiology is argued

# Statistical mechanics of mechanically adaptive cytoskeletal organization

Minimal equilibrium statistical-mechanical model of the cytoskeleton: cytoskeletal
architectures are Boltzmann ensembles of microscopic configurations, and a free
energy balancing configurational entropy against mechanically tuned interaction
energies is minimized as a scalar tension parameter (rising with substrate
stiffness) is swept. Three successive transitions appear — stress-fibre formation,
alignment, lateral aggregation — reproducing the stiffness-ordered sequence and
the correlation-length / anisotropy trends measured in senescent fibroblasts. The
paper reads as an order-disorder / nematic-ordering problem in biological
vocabulary. Only the abstract was available; card at low confidence, and the
specific model form (interaction energy, degrees of freedom, whether the
transitions are singular) is not recoverable from it.
