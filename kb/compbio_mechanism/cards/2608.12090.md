  Protein language models (PLMs) are used as feature extractors: an amino-acid
  sequence is mapped to embeddings that feed downstream predictors. By

  fluorescence, homology). Datasets split into deep-mutational-scan (DMS) type
  (many variants of one or few proteins) and diverse-natural-protein type.

method: >
  For every PLM and every layer, train a probe model on that layer's embeddings
  for each downstream task and compare probe performance across layers. In
  parallel, compute geometric descriptors of each layer's latent space
  (intrinsic dimension, neighborhood overlap between consecutive layers) to
  characterize how representations evolve with depth. Relate the layer-vs-
  performance profile to (i) similarity between the masked-language-modeling
  pretraining objective and the downstream objective and (ii) dataset type.
  Repeat on artificial-protein tasks.
contribution: >
  Systematic evidence that the last layer of a PLM is rarely the best layer for
  a downstream task. Identifies two determinants of where the useful
  information sits: objective mismatch (residue-level tasks, close to the MLM
  objective, improve monotonically with depth; whole-protein tasks do not) and
  dataset type (DMS datasets are best served by shallow layers, diverse-protein
  datasets by deep layers). Shows PLM performance drops sharply on artificial
  proteins even when they are verified functional, arguing PLMs accumulate an
  abstraction of naturally evolved sequence space, not of protein function per se.

# Task- and dataset-specific information in protein language models

Layer-wise probing of 13 PLMs on 15 downstream tasks, plus latent-space geometry
(intrinsic dimension, neighborhood overlap). The last layer is rarely the best
layer. Two determinants: objective mismatch (residue-level tasks track the MLM
objective and improve monotonically with depth; whole-protein tasks peak
earlier) and dataset type (DMS datasets favor shallow layers, diverse-protein
datasets favor deep layers). PLM performance collapses on verified-functional
artificial proteins, suggesting PLMs abstract naturally evolved sequence space
rather than protein function. Findings are correlational and the two determinants
co-vary; actionable output is a layer-selection procedure requiring labelled
validation data.
