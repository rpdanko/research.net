  Evolutionary Curriculum Learning (ECL): a plug-in change to minibatch
  sampling only, no architecture change. Pick a random anchor sequence; draw
  minibatches from its neighborhood of evolutionarily similar sequences; expand
  that neighborhood over training according to a power-law schedule, so the
  model sees easy (locally consistent, close) sequences first and hard
  (divergent) sequences later. Pairwise evolutionary distances are computed once
  in preprocessing. Ablated against fixed-size neighborhood sampling and uniform
  random sampling.

# Evolutionary Curriculum Learning for MSA-based sequence VAEs

ECL changes only minibatch sampling for MSA-trained VAEs: sample an anchor
sequence, draw minibatches from an evolutionarily-close neighborhood, and expand
that neighborhood over training on a power-law schedule (easy/close sequences
first, divergent ones later). No architecture change; needs an O(N^2)
pairwise-distance preprocessing pass. Applied to EVE (protein variant effect) and
RfamGen (RNA generation), it improves downstream metrics in a consistent
direction across five seeds, and ablations show progressive expansion beats
fixed-size and uniform sampling. The paper explicitly frames MSA rows as
non-exchangeable (latent tree structure) and uses evolutionary distance as the
curriculum difficulty measure. Authors are candid that effect sizes are small
relative to seed variance and most single comparisons are not significant.
