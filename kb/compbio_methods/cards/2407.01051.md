problem: >
  Accelerated first-order methods are complexity-optimal for convex smooth
  minimization, but many settings provide only an inexact gradient, and standard
  accelerated schemes accumulate that inexactness in their convergence bounds.
  Can subspace-optimization methods, which are known not to accumulate gradient
  error, be given a full complexity account that also charges for solving their
  low-dimensional subproblems, and can they be extended beyond convexity?
setting: >
  Convex smooth minimization with an L-Lipschitz gradient, and its relaxation to
  gamma-quasar-convex (weakly quasi-convex) functions satisfying a quadratic-growth
  / Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition. The gradient is available only with bounded
  additive noise of level delta_1; the inner subspace subproblems are solved
  inexactly to a controlled function-value accuracy. Deterministic analysis;
  numerical experiments including a non-convex quasar-convex problem.
method: >
  Modifications of two subspace-optimization schemes: Nemirovski's conjugate
  gradient method and Sequential Subspace Optimization (SESOP), each run with an
  inexact gradient. The inner low-dimensional subproblems are solved by the
  ellipsoid method or by a multidimensional dichotomy, and the subproblem accuracy
  is linked explicitly to the inexactness conditions so the total number of
  (full-dimensional) gradient evaluations, outer plus inner, can be bounded. A
  restart scheme and a stopping rule handle the quasar-convex case.

  gradient noise for quasar-convex functions under quadratic growth, with a stopping
  rule trading solution quality against iteration count; a total gradient-call
  complexity for SESOP and CG when the subproblems are convex, showing the inner
  solves (ellipsoid / dichotomy) do not materially inflate cost and the schemes
  stay first-order; and confirmation that neither method accumulates the gradient
  inexactness, converging to an O(delta_1^2/mu) noise floor.

# Subspace optimization methods with inexact gradients

Complexity and convergence analysis of Nemirovski's conjugate gradient method and
SESOP run with additively noisy gradients. Contributions: restarted CG converges
linearly for quasar-convex functions under quadratic growth (with a stopping rule
for solution quality); solving the inner low-dimensional subproblems by the
ellipsoid method or a dichotomy does not spoil the total first-order gradient-call
complexity when those subproblems are convex; and neither method accumulates the
gradient error, settling at an O(delta_1^2/mu) noise floor.
