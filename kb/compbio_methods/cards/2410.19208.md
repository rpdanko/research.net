problem: >
  Projecting an n-by-n symmetric matrix onto the positive semidefinite cone is a
  core subroutine (in SDP solvers, correlation-matrix repair, Lyapunov solutions),
  but the exact projection needs a full eigendecomposition at O(n^3) cost, which is
  intractable for large n. Can an accurate approximate projection be computed in
  sub-cubic time with a usable error guarantee?
setting: >
  Real symmetric matrices, Frobenius-norm projection onto the PSD cone. The working
  assumption is that the matrices of interest have rapidly decaying singular values
  so a low-rank surrogate has small truncation error. A user-chosen rank parameter
  k << n controls an accuracy/speed trade-off. Analysis gives probabilistic (not
  worst-case deterministic) error bounds; experiments on matrices up to n ~ 20,000.
method: >
  Two randomized numerical linear algebra algorithms. The first draws a random
  sketch of the matrix, forms a low-rank approximation, and applies an exact
  eigendecomposition to the resulting small matrix, then reconstitutes the
  projection. The second first rescales the matrix so that the leading singular
  values line up with the largest positive eigenvalues, biasing the sketch toward
  the subspace that survives PSD projection and away from large negative
  eigenvalues that are zeroed anyway. Both cost O(k n^2) and use a single pass.
contribution: >
  A randomized approximate PSD projection at O(k n^2) instead of O(n^3), in one
  pass over the matrix (unlike Krylov-based approximations that need multiple passes
  and suffer finite-precision instability); a scaling variant that improves accuracy
  by aligning the sketch with positive eigenspace; and probabilistic error bounds
  showing the error decreases as the sketch rank k grows, with large-scale numerical
  validation.

# Randomized approximate projection onto the PSD cone

Two randomized-numerical-linear-algebra algorithms that approximate the Frobenius
projection of a symmetric matrix onto the PSD cone in O(k n^2) single-pass time
instead of O(n^3). The first sketches to low rank then eigendecomposes the small
matrix; the second first rescales so leading singular values align with the
positive eigenspace. Probabilistic error bounds decreasing in the sketch rank k,
tested at n ~ 20,000. Analyzed as a standalone subroutine, not inside an SDP solver.
