### This is the 2407.01051 mistake, repeated

On card 1 I read v1 HTML, found no numerical-experiments section, and reported a

That work **does not cover this case**, and this case shows why. Pinning stamps

pinned to `…v1` while the abstract shown next to it during labelling came from a

Three sources, two of them unpinned:

| What | Where from | Version recorded? |
|---|---|---|
| e-print text the curator parsed | `ingest/cache/eprint/<id>.bin` | **yes**, since 2026-08-28 |
| abstract the curator read, and that `label` shows you | `papers.sqlite` | **no** |
| whatever I or you fetch to check | arxiv.org, live | **no** — resolves to latest |

Concrete fixes, in the order I'd do them:

1. **`arxiv_pull.py` should store the version with the abstract.** It already
   parses the API response that carries it; it discards it. One column, one
   regex — the same `_resolve_version` already written for `pdf_extract.py`.
2. **`card_eval.py label` should warn when the abstract's version and the card's
   `source_version` differ**, or when the abstract's version is unknown. Right
   now it prints the pinned e-print version with "CHECK AGAINST THIS VERSION"
   directly above an abstract that may not be from that version.
3. **Any verdict of `n` justified by absence needs its source version stated.**
   Twice now I have asserted "X is not in the paper" when I meant "X is not in
   the copy I fetched." That is a `[prior]`-class error wearing `[source]`
   clothing, and it is the single most dangerous shape of mistake in this whole
   exercise, because absence-based reasoning is exactly what a fabrication check
   is made of.

Worth stating plainly: on both occasions the card was right and the check was
wrong. Two for two. Whatever else this eval measures, it has not yet caught the

## The prose fields, with sources

### `problem` — **y**

Card: *"When can the latent component distributions, and the mixing matrix, of
several unlabeled finite mixtures that share components but have unknown mixing
weights be recovered, using only the assumption that each component is
independent on at least one coordinate pair?"*

> **[HTML §1, para. 2]** "We consider several observable mixtures that share the
> same latent components but have unknown mixing weights. Instead of assuming
> labeled samples, anchor sets, parametric component families, or known
> proportions, we assume that each component is marginally independent for at
> least one pair of coordinates. The goal is to recover the latent components
> and, when possible, the mixing matrix from the observable mixtures."

Sentence-for-sentence. The card's "using only" corresponds to the paper's
"Instead of assuming … we assume", and its pairing of components *and* mixing
matrix matches "recover the latent components and, when possible, the mixing
matrix". **Check this by reading one paragraph.**

### `method` — **y**, four clauses, four sources

**Clause 1** — "any fully independent distribution in the affine span of
product-form components must coincide with a single component, so the
intersection of the affine mixture space with the independence model is
discrete":

> **[HTML §1, para. 3]** "For product-form components, if the univariate
> marginals are linearly independent, then any fully independent distribution in
> the affine span of the components must coincide with one of the components.
> Thus, **the intersection between the affine mixture space and the independence
> model is discrete** and consists only of the component distributions."

This is almost word-for-word. Also **Theorem 1** (§3).

**Clause 2** — "extended to coordinate-pair (marginal) independence under
no-cancellation and full-rank conditions":

> **[HTML §1, para. 4]** "For a fixed coordinate pair, we show that any affine
> combination of the observable mixtures whose bivariate marginal is independent
> must recover a component that is independent on that pair. **Under a
> no-cancellation condition and linear independence of the relevant marginals**,
> the coordinate-pair independence constraint identifies the corresponding
> components."

And the condition is named as such at **Remark 5** (§4):

> "The assumption (6) in Theorem 2 is a **generic no-cancellation condition**. It
> rules out signed affine cancellations in which dependent non-target components
> combine with target components to produce a signed product-form marginal."

**Clause 3** — "PM-MMD … comparing a candidate's bivariate marginal with the
product of its one-dimensional marginals via a characteristic kernel, minimized
over signed affine combinations":

> **[HTML §1, para. 5]** "We introduce a Product-Marginal Maximum Mean
> Discrepancy (PM-MMD) criterion: for a candidate affine combination of the
> observable mixtures, we compare its bivariate marginal with the product of its
> one-dimensional marginals **using a characteristic kernel**."

"Signed" is confirmed by the search domain definition in §5:

> "$\Delta_{\bar r} := \{r \in \mathbb{R}^L \mid r^T\mathbf{1} = 1, \|r\|_1 \le
> \bar r\}$, where $\bar r$ is a large constant… **The non-negativity constraint
> $r \ge 0$ is not imposed in general.**"

That last sentence is the one that makes "signed affine combinations" (rather
than "convex combinations") exactly right, and it is a real distinction — a
curator writing "convex" there would have been wrong.

**Clause 4** — "Kernel mean embedding deviation bounds give uniform convergence
and stability":

> **[Theorem 3, §5]** "…with probability at least $1-\delta$,
> $\sup_{r\in\Delta_{\bar r}}\left|\mathrm{MMD}(r^TU)-\mathrm{MMD}(r^T\widehat
> U)\right| \le 10\bar r^2 K(1+\sqrt{\log(2L^2/\delta)})N_{1/2}$."
>
> and immediately after: "This result shows that the empirical MMD criterion is
> **uniformly consistent** on $\Delta_{\bar r}$."

The `sup` over the whole simplex is what makes it *uniform* rather than
pointwise. Stability is **Theorems 4 and 5**, which show the level set
$\{d(r)\le c\}$ splits into $|R_{12}|$ disjoint pieces each containing exactly
one solution, and that this survives passing to the empirical $\widehat d$.

### `contribution` — **y**, three claims

**Claim 1**, identifiability + mixing matrix under completion conditions:

> **[HTML §1, para. 4]** "If every component is independent for at least one
> coordinate pair, all components are identifiable. **Under additional
> irreducibility or square-completion conditions, the mixing matrix is also
> recoverable.**"

> **[HTML §1, para. 5]** "In the square case, we complete the mixing matrix
> directly when all weight vectors are recovered, and provide partial-recovery
> completion rules for the cases where one or two components remain unresolved."

**Claim 2**, PM-MMD with proven convergence and stability — Theorems 3/4/5 above.

**Claim 3**, the separation of empirical status. This is the card's sharpest
sentence and it tracks a whole section, **§4.3 "Testability of the
Assumptions"**:

> "Marginal independence gives an observable restriction on an affine candidate
> formed from the observed mixtures. Irreducibility and anchor-type conditions,
> on the other hand, are assumptions on latent components or residual
> distributions. They are used as identification assumptions, rather than as
> directly testable conditions."

> "In this paper, irreducibility and anchor-type conditions should therefore be
> read as latent-decomposition assumptions… **they should not be presented as
> directly testable from the unlabeled mixtures alone.** By contrast, marginal
> independence provides an observable product-marginal check, and Section 5
> introduces an MMD-based criterion for carrying out this check from samples."

The card's "held-out" qualifier is also the paper's:

> **[HTML §1, para. 6]** "PM-MMD is used as a **held-out check**: small values
> suggest compatibility with marginal independence, whereas large values suggest
> that the candidate lacks the required product-marginal structure."

### Object 1 is the only real defect, and you can check it without the paper

`Finite mixture model` carries `aliases: [mixing matrix]`. A mixing matrix is a
**constituent** of a finite mixture model — the matrix $\Theta$ of weights — not
another name for it. The card's own role text says so: *"shared latent components
combined with unknown per-mixture weight vectors **collected in the mixing
matrix**."* The role describes them as different things; the alias asserts they
are the same thing.

`aliases` feeds concordance merging, so this collapses two distinct nodes into
one. Same error class as card 1's `Ellipsoid method` aliased to *"multidimensional
dichotomy (as alternative inner solver)"* — a related object filed as a synonym.

**This needs no domain knowledge and no paper access.** The contradiction is
between two fields of the same card.

### Note surviving on object 6

Still worth recording, now as a naming observation rather than an error: the name
bundles two conditions the paper treats separately. **Remark 6** shows they are
independent — "If $R_{st} = [m]$… the additional assumption … in Theorem 2 is
automatically satisfied" — i.e. no-cancellation becomes vacuous in a case where
the rank condition still bites. One concordance node now stands for two
conditions. Compare `Hermitization (Girko's method)` on card 2410.16457: the same
"one name doing two jobs" shape, from a different cause.

### `objects_missed`: **1** (revised down from 2)

**Irreducibility.** It is formally defined in §4.3 ("We say that F is irreducible
with respect to H if…"), it is how the mixing matrix gets completed — half of
what the title promises — and it appears in this card's *own* `contribution` and
`limitations` fields. No object carries it.

I previously counted anchor conditions as a second miss. Demoting that: the paper
introduces them mainly as a **contrast class** in §4.3 and in related work, not as
machinery this paper's results run on. One solid miss.
