### `problem` — **y**

### `setting` — **y**

### `method` — **p**

Most of it is exact: "finite-budget planning over hallucinated sequence-structure
states" (Q6, Q9); nodes as states (Q9); the value signal combining confidence,
uncertainty and cross-expert consensus/disagreement (Q13, Q14); "optional
biophysical feasibility terms (steric, thermodynamic, energy-like)" tracks Q8
almost word for word; "no component model is fine-tuned or differentiated
through" is the abstract.

**The defect: "Monte Carlo Tree Search runs selection / expansion / simulation /
backup."**

Q10 names the four phases as **selection, expansion, evaluation, backpropagation**
— and goes out of its way to say evaluations come from model-derived value
signals "**rather than long stochastic rollouts**." Figure 1 labels them
1. Selection, 2. Expansion, **3. Evaluation**, 4. Backpropagation.

"Simulation" is the textbook MCTS phase name — the rollout step this paper
explicitly does not perform. The curator wrote the canonical four-phase cycle
from memory at precisely the point where the paper departs from it.

That is not a generic slip. It is the exact failure `card_eval.py`'s own
`sections_read` comment predicts:

> "standard-for-the-area detail was supplied from priors rather than extracted —
> which is right most of the time in a well-trodden area and **wrong exactly
> where a paper deviates from the standard scheme, i.e. where the novelty is.**"

`sections_read` here is `[abstract, intro, conclusion]`. Q10 is in §2. The
curator could not have read it, and filled the gap with the default.

### `contribution` — **y**

for multi-expert consensus/disagreement; Q16 and the abstract for held-out

### `limitations` — **p**

Bullets 1, 3, 4, 5 hold. Bullet 1 is Q18 near-verbatim, including that wet-lab
validation is named as future work. Bullets 3 and 5 are carefully scoped to "the
extract", which is honest and correct.

**Bullet 2 is wrong**: *"there is no comparison to gradient-based hallucination
(BindCraft, BoltzDesign) at matched wall-clock, only the argument that those are
expensive and initialization-sensitive."*

Q17: BoltzDesign1 **is** compared, at a matched budget of 100 folding/evaluator

The "at matched wall-clock" qualifier is technically a defence — the paper

reader BoltzDesign was never compared, and it was. BindCraft indeed is not

contain the evidence.** §4 holds the comparison; `sections_read` is abstract,

### Two `named_in_paper: false` claims, one right and one wrong

This is the first card carrying two, and it splits.

**Object 4 (query-by-committee) is correct and is a genuine win.** The paper
never says "query-by-committee" or "committee" anywhere in the main text. What
it does — Q14, plus eq (6)'s consensus prior and eq (8)'s $U_{\text{fold}} =
\mathcal{D}(\{X'^{(e)}\})$ — is exactly the QBC principle: ensemble agreement as
value, ensemble disagreement as the uncertainty signal steering where to spend
budget. Correctly identified, correctly flagged unnamed. Second such win in two
cards, after PMI on 2506.07459.

**Object 2 (UCT) is wrong, and its evidence field says so explicitly.** The card
writes *"the standard UCT selection mechanism, though **UCT/UCB is not named**."*
It is named — twice as "UCT-style" (Q11, Q12), and eq (4) is built on **PUCT** by
name (Q13).

The role is right: eq (4) is a Q-value plus a $c_p\sqrt{\log N(s)}/(1+N(s,a))$
visit-count exploration bonus, which is what the card describes. So the object is
real and correctly roled — by the letter, a `y`.

I score it **`p`** because the flag that is wrong is the one that decides how the
object is *scored*. `score_run()` computes named and unnamed precision
separately and calls the unnamed figure "the single most actionable number this
script produces". A false `named_in_paper: false` inflates exactly that number —
the system claims credit for recovering an unnamed object the paper names.

**But note where the three occurrences live**: Figure 1's caption and §3. None in
the abstract, intro, or conclusion. Against `sections_read`, the curator's claim
is *defensible*; against the paper, it is *false*.

**That is a schema ambiguity with a number attached.** `named_in_paper` does not
say whether it means "named in the paper" or "named in what I read", and here the
two answers differ and the metric depends on it. It should be settled explicitly,
and the honest resolution is probably to require that a `named_in_paper: false`
claim be made only after a full-text search — which is cheap now that
`show_eprint.py` exists.

### Object 7's flag is the one thing I could not check

unmistakably the paper's: "frozen black-box operators", "requires only model

parameters", "without gradients". But I did not see the phrase "derivative-free
optimization" or "zeroth-order optimization" anywhere in pp. 1–11. "Black-box"
appears, applied to *operators*, not to the optimization.

Appendices A–E are unread, so I am not scoring this on absence. Flagged as the
mirror image of object 2: possibly `true` where it should be `false`.
