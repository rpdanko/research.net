## 3. Verdicts

### `problem` — **y**

Both halves are Q5 and Q6, near-verbatim. "Capped by the small, biased pool" is
Q5's "data scarcity creates a ceiling" plus "limited diversity and natural
biases"; the objective mismatch is Q6 word for word, including the
designable/stable/diverse triple.

One `[prior]` element: the gloss "(sequence recovery)" for the supervised
objective. Q6 says "the supervised learning task of inverse folding" without
naming its loss. §3.1 defines it as conditional cross-entropy — outside
`sections_read`. The gloss is correct but inferred. Does not move the verdict.

### `setting` — **p**

Sourced: "fixed-backbone protein sequence design" (Q7), "online RL… no curated
preference dataset" (Q8, near-verbatim), CATH-4.3 and the three baselines (Q2,
Q3), "a full run fits on one 8-GPU node in three days" (Q3).

`[prior]`: "treated as a policy" — correct, but the word does not appear in
abstract or intro.

**The defect:** *"with held-out oracles (ESMFold, AlphaFold3, FoldX, Rosetta)."*

**ESMFold is not a held-out oracle. It is the training reward.** Q4: "proxy
**reward models** based on ESM-fold." Q9: "lightweight designability **rewards
provided by ESMFold**." The card's own `method` field says the same — "a
designability signal from ESMFold refolding" as reward component (i).

So `setting` lists ESMFold among held-out oracles while `method` lists it as the
reward being optimized. Those cannot both be true, and the distinction is the
one that decides whether the headline number means anything: a model tuned
against ESMFold and then evaluated by ESMFold has been evaluated on its training
signal. The paper is careful here — Appendix C is titled "Qualitative Evaluation
of ProteinZero using **AlphaFold3 and Rosetta**" precisely because those *are*
held out. The card blurs a distinction the paper draws.

**This is checkable with no paper access**: `setting` contradicts `method` on the
same card.

### `method` — **y**

The three-term objective is Q1, matching on all three terms and on
"protein-embedding level". Reward component (i) is Q9. "Avoiding physics-based
energy calculations" is Q14 (FoldX 472 s vs ~2 s). The algorithm sweep is Q11.

Two things reaching past Q9, both flagged rather than penalised:

- **"normalized by unconditional priors."** Q9 says the estimator is "based on
  inverse folding sequence likelihoods **and** unconditional sequence priors".
  "And" names two ingredients; "normalized by" asserts a **ratio**. The ratio is
  what makes the object-5 claim work, and v1's intro does not state it. See
  object 5.
- **"multi-round DPO"** as a swept algorithm. Q11 lists GRPO, RAFT, DPO only.
  Plausibly in §4.4, which I cannot see.

Neither is wrong; both are beyond what abstract+intro support.

### Object 3 is the worst alias failure across all four cards

`Group Relative Policy Optimization` carries `aliases: [GRPO, RAFT, DPO,
reward-ranked fine-tuning]`.

GRPO ✓. **RAFT and DPO are not aliases for GRPO — they are the other algorithms
it was compared against**, and the paper says so twice:

> Q11: "examining **different algorithms** (GRPO, RAFT, DPO)"
> Q12: "**online methods** like PPO, GRPO, and RAFT… and **offline methods like
> DPO** that learn from static preference datasets"

They are not even the same category — GRPO is online policy optimization, DPO is
offline preference learning. Listing them as synonyms collapses three distinct
methods into one concordance node, and the card's own role text half-admits it
by saying "the concrete policy-optimization **algorithms** compared" — plural,
under a singular name.

Same class as card 2407.01051's `Quadratic growth condition` aliased to the PL
inequality, which was scored `n`. Consistent verdict: **`n`**.

### Objects 2 and 4: the same defect, milder

Both have two correct aliases and one that names a *different* object:

- Object 2: `KL penalty` ✓, `KL regularization to reference policy` ✓, **`trust
  region` ✗** — a trust region is a constraint region; KL is a divergence used to
  define one. Method vs. quantity.
- Object 4: `multi-reward optimization` ✓ (Q1's phrase), `weighted-sum reward` ✓
  (that *is* scalarization), **`Pareto trade-off` ✗** — the Pareto frontier is
  what scalarization approximates, not another name for it.

Object 6's `entropy-style regularizer` is loose but *not* this error: it names a
family resemblance, not a specific object asserted as identical. Left at `y`.

### Object 5 — the `named_in_paper: false` claim, and why I can't settle it

This is the highest-value and least checkable field in the system, and it is the
first one to appear in four cards.

The claim: the ddG surrogate is $\log P(y \mid x) - \log P(y)$, which is exactly
pointwise mutual information between sequence and structure. **If that is the
formula, the identification is correct** — that expression is PMI by definition,
and recovering it under a biological name is precisely what compbio_mechanism
exists to do.

Two problems, neither of which makes it wrong:

1. **The `evidence` field quotes text I cannot locate.** It gives *"Intro
   contribution 2: 'a self-derived ddG estimator computed from the inverse
   folding model using backbone-conditioned likelihoods normalized by
   unconditional priors'"*. Q9 — the actual v1 contribution 2 — reads *"a fast
   thermodynamic stability ddG estimator based on inverse folding sequence
   likelihoods and unconditional sequence priors."* Different wording. Given the
   confirmed abstract discrepancy, the card's version is most likely a **later
   revision's intro**, and "self-derived" does appear in the DB abstract. I am
   **not** calling this fabricated — that is the mistake I made twice on the last
   card.

   But note what follows regardless of who is right: **an `evidence` quote with
   no version pin is not evidence.** The field exists to make an unverifiable
   claim verifiable, and it currently cannot do that job.

2. **The ratio is doing the work, and only the unlocatable quote asserts it.** Q9
   says "likelihoods **and** priors" — two ingredients, no combination rule.
   "Normalized by" asserts a quotient, and PMI needs the quotient. The formula
   lives in §3.2.2, which neither the curator (`sections_read: [abstract,
   intro]`) nor I have read.

So the curator inferred PMI from an intro sentence naming two ingredients,
without seeing the equation. The inference is very likely right. It is still an
inference presented as an extraction. **`p`** — and this is the single thing on
this card most worth your time.
